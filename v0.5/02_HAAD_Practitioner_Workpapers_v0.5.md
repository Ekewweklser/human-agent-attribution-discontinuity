## Classification key

### Evidentiary classifications

- **Known–established:** reliable evidence establishes the proposition and materially plausible alternatives have been addressed.
- **Known–contradicted:** reliable evidence establishes that the proposed transition did not occur as alleged.
- **Assumed:** used for a limited analytical purpose but not established; the assumption and effect are explicit.
- **Undetermined–pending:** potentially resolvable evidence remains to be obtained or tested.
- **Undetermined–exhausted, absent:** the necessary evidence was not created, retained, or is established not to exist.
- **Undetermined–exhausted, inaccessible:** evidence may exist, but reasonable acquisition avenues within authority and scope are exhausted.
- **Undetermined–exhausted, existence unknown:** reasonable inquiries cannot establish whether the evidence exists.

### Scope dispositions

- **Not in issue:** the dimension belongs to the model, but no material fact, context, or competing explanation places it in dispute.
- **Not applicable:** the dimension does not logically apply to the scoped attribution claim.

These are not evidentiary conclusions and do not mean known–established.

Record:

1. the affected attribution dimension;
2. the failure condition or conditions;
3. the graph location; and
4. whether the discontinuity is primary, co-primary, contributing, causal, or downstream.
